# APM / Entry-Level PM Market Research (India focus), 2025–2026

**Prepared:** 2026-10-09 · **Access date for all pages:** 2026-10-09 unless stated otherwise

## Method and caveats

- Pages were fetched live on 2026-10-09. Where a careers page could not be reached or had no relevant opening, the best accessible substitute is used and labelled **SUBSTITUTE**.
- Quoted phrases come from the fetched page text, passed through a page-reading tool. Short quotes are reproduced as returned. Text without quotation marks is a paraphrase.
- **Listing-only** means the title appeared on a careers search page but the detail page could not be opened. **Snippet-only** means the item was seen only in a search-result title or snippet.
- **Key structural finding:** Most Indian "APM" and "PM-1" postings that could be reached ask for 1–5 years of experience. The routes actually open to new graduates are campus programs: Google APM (University Graduate) and Flipkart's campus APM hiring. I found no India-based Amazon new-grad PM posting.

## 3. Top 8 skills by frequency

1. **Data and analytics fluency** (7/7). This is the most explicit requirement everywhere ("Extremely analytical and data driven", "analytical and data-driven approach").
2. **Customer empathy and user research** (7/7), e.g. "Proven customer champion" and "Be the voice of the customer".
3. **Cross-functional and stakeholder management** (7/7).
4. **Technical background** (7/7). Razorpay PM-I asks for "2 years of hands-on software development experience", and Swiggy wants a CS degree.
5. **Communication**, including written specs and presentations (7/7).
6. **Business acumen and GTM** (7/7): business cases, pricing, growth levers.
7. **Roadmapping and prioritization** (7/7, two of them implied).
8. **Shipping and end-to-end delivery record** (6/7), followed closely by **experimentation** (5/7) and **comfort with ambiguity** (5/7).

**Under-represented but differentiating:** AI/ML is named only by Google (preferred) and Amazon (ML products, ML applications). Both are the most selective programs. SQL is explicit only at Amazon, but "derive actionable conclusions from large data sets" (PhonePe) implies it in practice.

## 4. What strong APM portfolios and case studies contain

Sources used (Lenny's Newsletter, Reforge and Shreyas Doshi were searched, but I fetched no relevant first-hand article from them, so they are not cited):

- **Provn, "Product Manager Builder Portfolio: Prove Judgment" (2026-06-03).** A portfolio should prove "problem framing, customer insight, prioritization, prototype fluency, and measurable reasoning". "Three strong case studies usually beat eight thin project summaries." Each case should let "the reader… replay the product decision". "A prototype does not need production code. It does need behavior." Do not invent metrics. Disclose AI use: "say what the tool did, what the PM decided, and where human judgment changed the output." Hiring managers are "trying to separate polish from capability."
- **Aakash Gupta, Product Growth, "How to make a product manager portfolio" (2024-06-01).** Show "context, actions, and results" with "visual proof and examples". One hiring manager said a portfolio "gave me confirmation you can do the job". Interviews are "terribly low signal". The rest of the article is paywalled. **Aakash Gupta, "How to Become a PM" (2022-01-23):** "Build a product… list yourself… as the founder and product person", then write its vision, strategy, roadmap and specs.
- **Supra Insider #121 (2026-08-03, with Colin Matthews).** Side projects count if they are real, ideally with real users or revenue: "start with distribution, then work backward to the customers and problems you can reach". Prototype before writing the spec.
- **IIT Kanpur SPO placement insight, Isha Agrawal, APM at Pine Labs (2024).** Resume shortlisting is "a major step in getting a PM role". Two of three rounds grilled resume projects: "What was the user flow for this app you developed?" and the north-star metric. "STRUCTURE IS VERY IMPORTANT IN CASES!!"
- **Leland via University of Miami (2026-01-29), on Google APM.** "show real product thinking", "communicate technical concepts clearly", "Use data to support your points". Expect questions on "a product that you led from idea to launch".
- **Corroborating sources:** The Product Folks (2024-01-19) suggests opening a case study with the challenge, showing PRDs, wireframes and prototypes, and closing with "key metrics showcasing the product's success". Exponent (Hamsa Pillai, recorded 2019) says entry-level hiring rewards "raw potential than refined experience" and that tradeoff reasoning matters. Aced/Exponent (Stephen Cognetta, undated) suggests highlighting "Do you know SQL?" and qualitative and quantitative user research.

**Synthesis: a strong APM case study contains**
1. A sharp problem statement and a named user segment.
2. Evidence from real users (interviews, sessions).
3. Options considered and the explicit trade-offs.
4. A working prototype that tests the riskiest assumption.
5. A north-star metric, guardrail metrics and an experiment plan.
6. Honest results or measurement intent.
7. A written artifact, such as a PRD or one-pager.
8. A clear statement of AI-assisted versus human decisions.

## 5. Implications for a CS new grad with AI engineering internship experience

**Targeting.** Realistic new-grad doors are Google APM (2027 cycle listed. Check open windows, since the 2026 cycle ran 2025-09-30 to 2025-10-28) and Flipkart campus APM (through campus drives or GRiD/GWC). Most PhonePe, Razorpay, Swiggy and Amazon India PM roles want 1–5 years. Treat them as targets for referrals or for a future move. Razorpay's PM-I wants "hands-on software development experience", which makes an engineering-to-PM path credible.

**Your edge.** AI/ML familiarity is preferred only at the most selective employers (Google, Amazon), and the AI engineering internship covers it. Present the internship in PM terms: who the user was, what decision you influenced, which metric moved, and which trade-off you made. Google explicitly lists "Internship… in… software development" plus AI/ML application.

**ReviewRadar (customer-feedback intelligence for PMs)**
- Covers customer empathy, data and analytics, and AI/ML, three of the top skills. It also echoes Flipkart's "continuously improve the product based on customer feedback and data-driven insights".
- **Gap to close:** PMs are the users here. Interview 5–10 real PMs or founders, Indian startup PMs included, and document what they do today. Choose one wedge, such as clustering Play Store reviews into prioritized themes.
- Show measurable reasoning: precision of theme clustering against a human-labelled sample, time-to-insight against a manual baseline, and a north-star such as "insights acted on per week".
- Include a SQL or analytics layer and an evaluation write-up of where the LLM failed and how you guarded against it. Disclose AI use per Provn.
- Add a short PRD and a prioritization artifact (RICE or similar) to cover the "roadmap/prioritization" and "written communication" skills.

**Kamai (net-earnings app for Indian delivery riders)**
- This project lines up directly with Swiggy's PM-I posting, which manages "gig pickers" and wants a "bias for experimenting with incentives and policies (e.g. bonuses, penalties)". It is also relevant to Ola (mobility) and to PhonePe and Razorpay (payments and earnings flows).
- **Strongest evidence:** field interviews with riders, in Hindi or a regional language if possible. Document the true cost structure (fuel, EMI, platform commissions, idle time) and how riders decide which shifts to work. This is the "voice of the customer" evidence Flipkart and PhonePe ask for.
- Define a north-star metric, such as weekly active riders who log at least 5 shifts, along with a retention metric. Propose an A/B test, for example whether a daily earnings-per-hour nudge changes shift choice, with a hypothesis, sample size reasoning and guardrails. This covers experimentation (5/7) and data (7/7).
- Show India-scale constraints: low-end Android, intermittent data, vernacular UI, and UPI or SMS parsing with privacy trade-offs. Discuss business viability (who pays?) to cover business acumen (7/7).
- Follow the distribution-first advice: say how you reached riders (WhatsApp groups, hub visits), and report real usage, even if small.

**Portfolio packaging**
- Use two deep case studies rather than many thin ones. Each needs a one-line problem, users, evidence, trade-offs, a live prototype link, metrics, what you would do next, and an AI-disclosure note.
- Prepare to be grilled on both projects' user flows and north-star metrics, as in the IITK Pine Labs account.
- Prepare one technical presentation of each project, since Google lists presenting technical topics as a minimum qualification.
